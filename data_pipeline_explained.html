From Mainframe to Paycheck

How one quiet night of batch processing turns a day of sales into 14,212 commission statements.

Every morning around seven, 14,212 sales reps can open a statement that tells them exactly what they earned. The number looks simple. Getting it there takes a night of quiet, careful machinery that runs while everyone sleeps.

Let's follow that machinery from start to finish: where the data lives, how it's pulled out and cleaned, how a batch engine turns sales into payouts, where the results go next, and how they end up in front of people. Along the way we'll follow one order, #A-10293, all the way to a paycheck.

Scene 1 00:30

The system of record

It starts on the mainframe. For decades this z/OS host has been the source of truth: orders and their sales-credit splits in DB2, the employee and reporting hierarchy in VSAM, plan definitions with quotas and rate tables beside them.

At 00:30, after the day's transactions settle, COBOL batch jobs unload each table into a nightly flat file: fixed-width text with one record per line, plus a trailer record that says how many lines there should be.

key ideaThe mainframe doesn't push data anywhere. It just leaves tidy, counted files on the doorstep at the same time every night.
One line of ORDR.D261006.DAT, and it's our record ↓

The amount field is PIC 9(10)V99: the decimal point is implied, so 000004800000 means 48,000.00. Remember it, because the ETL is about to translate it.

Scene 2 01:00 – 02:20

Extract, cleanse, transform, validate

At 01:00 the ETL tool picks up the files. Extract reads them, and cleanse deals with the mess: stray spaces, inconsistent name casing, duplicate keys.

Transform turns host formats into modern ones. Implied decimals become real numbers, DDMONYY dates become ISO dates, and codes are looked up against the hierarchy.

Validate is the bouncer. Every row goes either into staging or into the reject file with a reason attached. Nothing just disappears.

a math moment

A conservation law for rows

Here's the one equation the whole night depends on. Every row that comes in has to end up somewhere we can count. Rows work like energy: inside the pipeline they're never created or destroyed, only moved from one bucket to another.

rowsin=rowsloaded+rowsrejected 1,284,512=1,284,377+135

and the same idea for money: a hash total is the sum of the amount column

Σamountin=Σamountloaded+Σamountrejected $612,438,905.17=$612,401,118.42+$37,786.75

Row counts catch rows that went missing. Hash totals catch rows that changed along the way. Both have to balance before the next step is allowed to start.

1,284,512  ≠  1,284,376 + 135 one row unaccounted for → the job stops and pages the on-call engineer

Scene 3 02:30

Handing off to the batch engine

The staged files travel to the commission engine's landing zone over managed, encrypted file transfer.

A manifest rides along that lists every file, its row count and its SHA-256 fingerprint. The receiving side recomputes each fingerprint, and if they all match it writes a tiny trigger file.

key ideaThat empty .ok file is the starting gun. The scheduler is watching for it, and the moment it appears the calculation chain begins.

Scene 4 03:00 – 04:55

The calculation engine

From 03:00 a chain of scheduled batch jobs runs in strict order. Each job reads the previous job's output, checks its control totals, then writes its own.

Crediting decides who gets credit for each order. Rollups copy that credit up the hierarchy so managers and directors see team totals. Quota attainment divides credit by quota. The rate table turns attainment into a rate, and payout multiplies it all out.

our recordOrder A-10293 ($48,000) is credited 100% to J. Rivera, then rolls up to M. Okafor, R. Haddad and A. Lindqvist.

a second math moment

The payout formula

Strip away the job names and the engine computes one sum for each person r:

payoutr= Σc ∈ Cr ac·ρ(Ar)
Ar= Σ acQr

a is the credited amount, Q is the quota, A is attainment, and ρ is the rate for whichever tier A lands in.

Attainment tells you which step of the staircase you're standing on. Cross 100% of quota and every new dollar of credit earns 6% instead of 4%.

Real plans add accelerators, caps, splits and retro adjustments, but all of them are decorations on this one sum.

A = $1,348,800$1,200,000 = 112.4% ⟹ ρ(112.4%) = 6% (tier 3)
payoutA-10293 = $48,000 × 0.06 = $2,880.00

Scene 5 00:00 – 08:00

One night, eight hours

Here's the whole night on one clock. Each job starts only after the one before it has finished and reconciled, so the schedule reads like a relay race. The gap before 07:00 is on purpose: it's the restart buffer. If a step fails at 03:40, there's still time to fix it and rerun before anyone logs in.

Scene 6 05:00

Sending the results back

At 05:00 the engine publishes. The payout file goes back to the mainframe host, where payroll and general-ledger posting have run for decades.

Other systems get their own slice of the results: earnings for Payroll, accrual journals for Finance, attainment for HR and CRM, the full result set for the data warehouse, and a frozen, write-once snapshot for audit.

key ideaEvery receiver replies with an ACK file that echoes the count and total it received. Until the ACK arrives, the send isn't finished.

Scene 7 all night

Control points and failure handling

Things do break. Files arrive late, a code table is missing, a disk fills up. So the pipeline isn't designed never to fail. It's designed to stop safely, get fixed and carry on without anyone getting paid twice.

i.Reconcile at every hop

Counts and money totals are checked at the host trailer, the ETL reconciliation, the transfer hash, the calc control totals and the downstream ACKs. Any mismatch halts the chain right there.

ii.Restart, don't rerun

Every step writes a checkpoint. If PAYOUT ends with RC=12, ops fixes the cause and restarts from that step. Steps are idempotent: rerunning one replaces its output rather than adding to it, so nobody gets paid twice.

iii.Quarantine, don't block

Bad records go to an exception queue with a reason code, and the rest of the night carries on. If total payouts swing more than ±15% from last period, they're held until Finance approves them.

Scene 8 07:00

Reports for the people who need them

By 07:00 the warehouse is loaded and the reporting mart is refreshed. Everyone sees the same numbers, cut the way their job needs them.

Reps get a statement with every credited deal, its rate and what it earned. Managers see their team's attainment. Finance sees accruals by region, ready for the journal. Ops sees exactly what was rejected and why.

The whole picture

Here's everything on one page: five systems, one night, and six control points that keep the numbers honest.

hostETLtransfercalc enginedownstreamreportingcontrol point (count + total check)
  • The host keeps the truth and leaves counted, fixed-width files at 00:30.
  • ETL cleans and validates, and rowsin = rowsloaded + rowsrejected must hold.
  • A fingerprinted transfer and a trigger file start the batch engine at 03:00.
  • The engine credits, rolls up, measures attainment and computes Σ a·ρ(A) for every payee.
  • Results go back to the host and out to Payroll, GL, HR, CRM, the warehouse and the archive, and each one sends back an ACK.
  • Reports go live at 07:00, and every number on them traces back to a line in a file.
